# Content Strategy

Last updated: 27 September 2026

## Content objective

Turn a broad record of education, employment, and projects into a focused story: Passapol identifies real organisational needs and delivers practical, human-centred digital solutions with measurable outcomes.

## Messaging hierarchy

1. **Value:** Translate complex needs into useful digital and AI solutions.
2. **Credibility:** United Nations, KBTG, AOT, KMUTT, and UCD experience.
3. **Proof:** 6 hours saved per week in briefing preparation, an initial humanitarian document task reduced from more than ten minutes manually to approximately two minutes assisted, and positive classroom feedback on TARA.
4. **Method:** Business analysis, user research, design thinking, agile delivery, stakeholder engagement, and applied AI.
5. **Motivation:** Responsible technology that strengthens institutions, education, and social impact.

## Content pillars

### 1. Digital transformation in practice

Show how Passapol aligns people, processes, and technology. Use the UN Thailand political intelligence digest and AOT digital recommendations as evidence.

### 2. Responsible and useful AI

Focus on the organisational problem, safeguards, users, and adoption rather than model terminology. Use the UN humanitarian automation and KBTG virtual-patient work.

### 3. Education and social impact

Use TARA as the clearest end-to-end product story, supported by the live-pilot result and faculty validation.

### 4. International collaboration

Demonstrate cross-cultural work across Thailand, Viet Nam, Czech Republic, the United States, and Ireland. Keep flags decorative and never use them in place of location names.

Keep the political intelligence digest and AOT website recommendations as supporting work. Their individual pages now contain verified process details.

## Case-study template

Each case study should fit this structure:

1. **Summary:** one sentence with the user, problem, and result.
2. **Context:** organisation, dates, team, and constraints.
3. **My role:** specific responsibilities and collaborators.
4. **Challenge:** the user or organisational need.
5. **Approach:** research, decisions, and delivery process.
6. **Solution:** what was produced, in plain language.
7. **Outcome:** verified metrics, validation, or current status.
8. **Reflection:** one lesson or trade-off.
9. **Evidence:** approved screenshots, links, media, or references.

If a detail is confidential, say so once and explain the work at a safe level. Never manufacture substitute metrics.

## Voice and editorial rules

- Clear, warm, and evidence-led.
- Use first person for decisions and reflection; use active voice.
- Explain acronyms on first use, including retrieval-augmented generation (RAG).
- Separate individual contribution from team outcomes: use "led," "co-developed," or "contributed" precisely.
- Preserve qualifiers such as "estimated," "pilot," and sample sizes.
- Prefer short paragraphs, descriptive headings, and meaningful link text.
- Avoid phrases such as "cutting-edge," "revolutionary," "expert in everything," and unsupported future claims.
- Use British English to match the current Ireland/UN context.

## Maintenance

- Review dates, role status, CV, and contact links every three months.
- Add a new case study only when it has a clear role, approved evidence, and a defensible outcome.
- Replace an older secondary item rather than expanding the homepage indefinitely.
- Keep LinkedIn as the activity channel; no portfolio blog is needed for the MVP.
